For travel in South Korea, start with **annyeonghaseyo** (“hello”), **gamsahamnida** (“thank you”), **sillyehamnida** (“excuse me”), and **eolmayeyo?** (“how much is it?”). Keep **Hangul**, the Korean writing, beside the romanization so you can show the phrase if speaking does not work.

Polite wording is useful for a first meeting. Learn a complete expression with its ending rather than copying the shortest version from an informal conversation or entertainment clip.

<h2 id="polite">Learn polite greetings as whole phrases</h2>

| English | Korean | Romanization |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hello | <span lang="ko">안녕하세요</span> | Annyeonghaseyo |
| Thank you | <span lang="ko">감사합니다</span> | Gamsahamnida |
| Excuse me | <span lang="ko">실례합니다</span> | Sillyehamnida |
| I'm sorry | <span lang="ko">죄송합니다</span> | Joesonghamnida |
| Yes | <span lang="ko">네</span> | Ne |
| No, politely | <span lang="ko">아니요</span> | Aniyo |

Romanization makes the text approachable, but it is not English phonetic spelling. Korean sounds change in context, so listen to complete phrases when practising. The written **합니다** ending is commonly romanized **hamnida**, reflecting its spoken form rather than a letter-for-letter reading.

The [Seoul Tourism Organization's guide](https://english.visitseoul.net/etiquette) recommends starting politely. It also describes giving and receiving objects with both hands. When showing a booking or passing something across a counter, take your time and keep the interaction clear.

<h2 id="requests">Ask a short question or point to an item</h2>

| English | Korean | Romanization |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How much is it? | <span lang="ko">얼마예요?</span> | Eolmayeyo? |
| This, please | <span lang="ko">이거 주세요</span> | Igeo juseyo |
| Water, please | <span lang="ko">물 주세요</span> | Mul juseyo |
| Where is the bathroom? | <span lang="ko">화장실 어디예요?</span> | Hwajangsil eodiyeyo? |

**Juseyo** is useful in a request for something. Point to the item while saying **igeo juseyo**, then check any quantity or size. A clear gesture towards the menu item gives the other person information that a phrase such as “this” cannot supply by itself.

When asking a price, confirm the amount on the display or in writing. Learning the question does not teach you every number you may hear in response. Keep your first phrase practice focused on a small request you can identify clearly.

<h2 id="writing">Keep Hangul names and addresses available</h2>

Save your accommodation's name and address in Korean from its own booking information. Keep the English version alongside it. That gives someone an exact destination to read rather than a romanized name they must interpret.

For a journey, save the station and destination as well as the accommodation. If a place has several entrances or nearby stops, keep the detail from your booking or route plan. A language phrase cannot resolve an ambiguous destination by itself.

Learn to recognise the Hangul for your most-used phrase even before learning the whole writing system. Finding **감사합니다** quickly on a saved card is a useful first step. The [National Institute of Korean Language dictionary](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/eng/mainAction) is a reference for further word meanings and pronunciation.

<h2 id="help">Ask for help when the exchange gets difficult</h2>

**도와주세요 — dowajuseyo** means “please help me.” Show the booking, location or item you need help with so the request has a clear context. You do not have to explain the entire problem in one sentence.

If you cannot follow a reply, pause and show the written information. Let the person finish before starting another question. For a time, price or platform, check the actual number together rather than assuming that recognising one word means you understood the whole answer.

<h2 id="practise">Prepare one exchange for your first day</h2>

Choose a greeting, an item request and a thank-you. Practise the sequence aloud, then practise showing the Hangul text. Save your first destination separately so it is easy to find at a counter or in transit.
