For a trip to Spain, start with **hola**, **por favor**, **gracias**, **perdone**, **¿habla inglés?**, and **no entiendo**. Then learn **la cuenta, por favor** for the bill and **un billete** for a transport ticket. These small requests are easier to practise than a long list of words unrelated to your itinerary.

Keep the Spanish line and its English meaning together on your phone. You can point to the phrase when a crowded café or unfamiliar accent makes speaking difficult.

<h2 id="first-phrases">Begin a short conversation</h2>

| What you want to say | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| Hello | Hola |
| Good morning | Buenos días |
| Please | Por favor |
| Thank you | Gracias |
| Excuse me, politely | Perdone |
| Do you speak English? | ¿Habla inglés? |
| I don't understand | No entiendo |
| More slowly, please | Más despacio, por favor |

Spanish **h** is silent, so **hola** starts with a vowel sound. For **por favor**, a rough English hint is “por fah-VOR.” Such hints only get you started; use a dictionary's audio to hear complete words and practise your own greeting plus question together.

**¿Habla inglés?** is addressed politely to one person. Wait for the answer before adding your request. If the reply is too fast, **más despacio, por favor** gives you a simple way to ask for a slower pace.

<h2 id="food-and-bills">Order and settle the bill</h2>

| At a café or restaurant | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| A table for two, please | Una mesa para dos, por favor |
| A coffee, please | Un café, por favor |
| Water, please | Agua, por favor |
| The bill, please | La cuenta, por favor |
| Can I pay by card? | ¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta? |
| How much is it? | ¿Cuánto cuesta? |

Use a menu entry to identify what you want, then add the quantity. If you need a particular kind of coffee, show the name on the menu instead of assuming that the word **café** specifies every detail of your order.

Watch the word **menú**. It can mean the list of dishes, but it can also refer to a **set menu**, such as a **menú del día**. Check the listed courses, price and inclusions before ordering. [SpanishDictionary's entry](https://www.spanishdict.com/translate/men%C3%BA) explains both meanings.

Our [menu translation guide]({{ '/blog/translate-menu-iphone/' | relative_url }}) can help with unfamiliar descriptions. Translating a dish and confirming what comes with a set menu are separate tasks; ask about the details you need.

<h2 id="tickets-and-signs">Ask for a ticket and recognise useful signs</h2>

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| One ticket to Madrid, please | Un billete a Madrid, por favor |
| Where is the station? | ¿Dónde está la estación? |
| Where is the toilet? | ¿Dónde está el baño? |
| Entrance / exit | Entrada / salida |
| Open / closed | Abierto / cerrado |

**Billete** is useful at a transport counter. **Entrada** also means an admission ticket, so the word on a museum booking may differ from the one on a train ticket. Show the date, destination and ticket type while asking; one short sentence does not establish whether you need a return or a particular service.

When someone gives directions, listen for **derecha** (“right”) and **izquierda** (“left”). If the route is still unclear, show your destination on a map and ask the person to indicate it. Confirming the place is more useful than pretending to follow a long spoken route.

<h2 id="spain-wording">Words you may hear differently in Spain</h2>

If you learned Spanish elsewhere, you may already know **jugo** for juice; **zumo** is useful to recognise in Spain. Likewise, a transport **billete** may be familiar to you as **boleto**. These are vocabulary differences, not reasons to discard the Spanish you already know.

You may also hear **vosotros**, an informal plural “you” used in much of Spain. Regional usage varies, as [FundéuRAE discusses](https://www.fundeu.es/consulta/tu-vos-usted-49/). A first-trip phrase card does not need to teach every pronoun or accent: practise a polite request you can use confidently with one person.

<h2 id="prepare">Save the phrases that match your plans</h2>

Choose six essentials, one restaurant request and your first transport question. Save the exact accommodation address and booking details alongside them. Practise saying a phrase, stopping, and opening the written version when you need help.
