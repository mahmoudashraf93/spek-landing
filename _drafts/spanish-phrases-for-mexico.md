For Mexico, learn **buenos días**, **por favor**, **gracias**, **con permiso**, **¿cuánto cuesta?**, and **no entiendo** first. Then add the food and transport requests you expect to use. You can communicate with ordinary polite Spanish; a beginner does not need to imitate slang to make a clear request.

Keep the phrase text visible on your phone. Local names, numbers and booking details are often the parts that need confirming, even when you know the sentence around them.

<h2 id="greetings">Start with polite, reusable Spanish</h2>

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| Good morning | Buenos días |
| Hello | Hola |
| Please | Por favor |
| Thank you | Gracias |
| Excuse me, to pass by | Con permiso |
| Do you speak English? | ¿Habla inglés? |
| I don't understand | No entiendo |
| Could you repeat that? | ¿Puede repetirlo? |

Use **con permiso** when you need to pass someone or move through a space. For getting someone's attention to ask a question, **disculpe** is another useful polite opening. The purpose of the phrase matters: an English “excuse me” can cover several situations.

The **h** in **hola** is silent. For pronunciation practice, use recorded dictionary audio and say a complete greeting plus request. A short line that you can retrieve comfortably is more useful than a longer sentence you have never spoken aloud.

<h2 id="food">Order an item and check the details</h2>

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| Two tacos, please | Dos tacos, por favor |
| Water, please | Agua, por favor |
| Without chilli, please | Sin chile, por favor |
| Is it spicy? | ¿Pica? |
| The bill, please | La cuenta, por favor |
| To go | Para llevar |

Point to the item or repeat its menu name while giving the quantity. **Para llevar** identifies a takeaway order. If there are several fillings or sizes, show the option you mean instead of expecting a general word such as “taco” to specify it.

**Sin chile** is a request about chilli, not a promise that a prepared dish contains none. Ask what can actually be changed. For ingredient requirements, discuss the dish with staff; a translated name does not establish how it was prepared.

One regional word you may encounter is **popote**, a drinking straw. [ASALE marks that meaning for Mexico](https://www.asale.org/damer/popote). It is useful vocabulary to recognise, without needing to replace every word you already know from another Spanish-speaking country.

<h2 id="prices">Ask the price and how you can pay</h2>

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| How much does it cost? | ¿Cuánto cuesta? |
| Can I pay by card? | ¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta? |
| Can I pay in cash? | ¿Puedo pagar en efectivo? |
| No, thank you | No, gracias |

Ask about the item and quantity together. Then confirm the final amount on the display or in writing. Recognising a price is a different skill from asking for it, and a number shown on a calculator is often easier to check than a rapid spoken answer.

Avoid turning your phrase card into a list of negotiating scripts you do not understand. A clear question, a confirmed amount and a polite **no, gracias** cover useful everyday decisions without requiring a debate.

<h2 id="transport">Tickets and a clear destination</h2>

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| One ticket to Puebla, please | Un boleto a Puebla, por favor |
| Where is the bus station? | ¿Dónde está la estación de autobuses? |
| Where is the bathroom? | ¿Dónde está el baño? |
| Right / left | Derecha / izquierda |
| Entrance / exit | Entrada / salida |

**Boleto** is a useful word for a transport or admission ticket in Latin America, as [SpanishDictionary explains](https://www.spanishdict.com/translate/boleto). If you learned **billete** in Spain, add **boleto** to your recognition vocabulary for this trip.

Keep the full destination address and terminal details from your booking. Show them while asking your question; the name of a city does not identify a particular terminal or accommodation. For a journey you have already booked, the departure time and service details are more useful than a freshly translated generic sentence.

<h2 id="fallback">Clarify the reply and save a backup</h2>

Use **no entiendo** when you lose the meaning, and **más despacio, por favor** for “more slowly, please.” Ask one question at a time. If you are still unsure, show the phrase, a map, or the amount you need to confirm.

Before leaving, save your first destination, a food order and six basic phrases offline in your notes. Try the sequence of asking a question, pausing, and showing the written version.
