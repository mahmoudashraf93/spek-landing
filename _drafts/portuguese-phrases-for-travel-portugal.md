For travel in Portugal, start with **bom dia**, **por favor**, **obrigado / obrigada**, **desculpe**, and **fala inglês?** Add **a conta, por favor** for the bill, **um bilhete** for a ticket, and **onde fica…?** to ask where a place is. These requests give you a small, useful European Portuguese phrase kit for your first day.

If you learned Brazilian Portuguese, keep using what you know. For this trip, add the Portugal vocabulary you will see on signs and hear around your bookings. You do not need to replace everything you have learned to ask for a coffee or directions.

<h2 id="basics">Start with polite basics</h2>

| English | Portuguese |
| --- | --- |
| Good morning | Bom dia |
| Good afternoon | Boa tarde |
| Good evening / good night | Boa noite |
| Please | Por favor |
| Thank you | Obrigado / obrigada |
| Excuse me | Desculpe |
| Do you speak English? | Fala inglês? |
| I don't understand | Não percebo |

**Obrigado** is traditionally used by a male speaker and **obrigada** by a female speaker; the ending refers to the person giving thanks. **Desculpe** is useful for opening a polite request for attention.

Portuguese spelling is not an English pronunciation system. Keep accents such as those in **não** and **inglês** when saving phrases, and listen to European Portuguese audio before practising. The sound of a fast answer may differ from the way a beginner expects the written words to sound.

<h2 id="cafe-hotel">At a café or your accommodation</h2>

| English | Portuguese |
| --- | --- |
| I'd like a table for two | Queria uma mesa para duas pessoas |
| The bill, please | A conta, por favor |
| I have a reservation under the name… | Tenho uma reserva em nome de… |
| What time is breakfast? | A que horas é o pequeno-almoço? |
| Where is the bathroom? | Onde fica a casa de banho? |

**Queria…** is a useful polite opening for a request. Learn it with the thing you will actually ask for rather than memorising the verb by itself. At the hotel, have the booking name written out so the receptionist can check it directly.

Two useful Portugal terms here are **pequeno-almoço** (“breakfast”) and **casa de banho** (“bathroom”). Save the breakfast time with your booking once you have the answer. It is easier to check a written time later than to reconstruct a number you heard during check-in.

The official [VisitPortugal phrase collection](https://www.visitportugal.com/en/sobre-portugal/frases-uteis) groups requests by travel situation. Choose the ones that match your bookings rather than trying to learn every section at once.

<h2 id="transport">Tickets, buses, and trains</h2>

| English | Portuguese |
| --- | --- |
| One ticket, please | Um bilhete, por favor |
| I'd like a return ticket | Queria um bilhete de ida e volta |
| Which bus goes to the centre? | Qual é o autocarro para o centro? |
| Which platform does the train to Porto leave from? | De que linha sai o comboio para o Porto? |
| Turn right / turn left | Vire à direita / vire à esquerda |
| Go straight ahead | Siga em frente |

In Portugal, recognise **comboio** for train and **autocarro** for bus. If you are used to **trem** and **ônibus**, these are useful additions to your travel vocabulary. **Bilhete de ida e volta** means a return ticket; confirm the actual fare and service before paying.

Keep the full destination and departure details visible when asking about a train. A phrase pattern cannot tell the other person which booking you hold. For directions on foot, show the address on a map and ask them to indicate the route if the spoken answer is hard to follow.

<h2 id="listen">Prepare for the reply as well as the question</h2>

Practise recognising **sim** (“yes”) and **não** (“no”), and rehearse **não percebo** when you need to stop and clarify. For a price or time, ask to see the amount on a display or in writing.

Try one complete exercise before travelling: greet, ask about breakfast, then record the answer in your notes. For a transport exercise, use your real destination name and booking. The phrase becomes easier to remember when it belongs to a specific plan.
