The first Japanese phrases to learn for travel are **sumimasen** (“excuse me”), **arigatō gozaimasu** (“thank you”), and **wakarimasen** (“I don't understand”). Add **doko desu ka?** for asking where something is and **ikura desu ka?** for asking its price. Keep the Japanese writing next to the romanization so you can show a phrase if speaking becomes difficult.

This is a starter kit for short interactions. It will not prepare you to understand every reply. A good travel phrase list should help you pause and clarify as well as ask questions.

<h2 id="polite-basics">Start politely with greetings and thanks</h2>

| English | Japanese | Romaji |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Good morning, politely | おはようございます | Ohayō gozaimasu |
| Hello / good afternoon | こんにちは | Konnichiwa |
| Good evening | こんばんは | Konbanwa |
| Excuse me | すみません | Sumimasen |
| Thank you, politely | ありがとうございます | Arigatō gozaimasu |
| I don't understand | わかりません | Wakarimasen |
| Yes | はい | Hai |
| No | いいえ | Iie |

The Latin letters are **romaji**, a way of writing Japanese sounds. A line above a vowel, as in **ō**, indicates a long vowel. Avoid reading the table as though every word follows English spelling rules; listen to Japanese learning audio before practising aloud.

Use **sumimasen** to open a request for someone's attention. Ask your question after the person responds rather than repeating the greeting over a longer sentence. For thanks to someone helping you, the longer **arigatō gozaimasu** is a useful polite form to learn as a unit.

[JNTO's language page](https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/japanese-language/) is a useful reference for the basic expressions. For structured listening practice, the Japan Foundation offers [Minato](https://minato-jf.jp/). The table here does not contain audio recordings.

<h2 id="small-questions">Ask about a price or a place</h2>

| English | Japanese | Romaji |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How much is it? | いくらですか？ | Ikura desu ka? |
| Where is the station? | 駅はどこですか？ | Eki wa doko desu ka? |
| Where is the toilet? | トイレはどこですか？ | Toire wa doko desu ka? |
| It's delicious | おいしいです | Oishii desu |

For directions, the pattern is **[place] wa doko desu ka?** The place name is what you change. Keep the actual name visible on your booking or map: “the station” may be too general when you need a particular station, entrance, or railway line.

When shopping, point to the item as you ask **ikura desu ka?** Then confirm the displayed amount. Knowing how to ask the price does not mean you will recognise a quickly spoken number. A calculator display or written total gives both people something concrete to check.

For food, point to the menu entry while discussing it. A picture and a dish name are more useful together than either alone. Our [iPhone menu translation guide]({{ '/blog/translate-menu-iphone/' | relative_url }}) explains ways to read unfamiliar menu text. Clarify ingredients with staff if they matter to your order.

<h2 id="written-backup">Keep names and addresses in Japanese</h2>

Save your accommodation's name and address **in Japanese**, alongside the English version. A screenshot of the booking or a copied address is a better fallback than inventing your own phonetic spelling. Save the exact destination before leaving your accommodation, especially when different places have similar English names.

Use two separate notes: one for phrases you want to say, and another for details you want to show. In the second, keep the hotel name, address, booking reference and nearest station together. You can hand over a clearly labelled destination without scrolling past a whole phrasebook.

If you do not understand a reply, use **wakarimasen** and show the written place or item. Restart with one request. Avoid agreeing just because you recognise **hai** somewhere in the exchange; check the action or amount you actually need to confirm.

<h2 id="practice">Practise one short exchange before you go</h2>

Choose a scenario from your first day: finding the station, checking a price, or thanking hotel staff. Say the opening, ask the question, then deliberately practise the point where you do not understand the answer. Open your saved note and show the Japanese text.
