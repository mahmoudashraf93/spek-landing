<h2 id="before-you-start">Which method should you try?</h2>

**Start with Camera or Photos.** On a supported iPhone, Apple’s Live Text can translate text on a menu without installing another app. Apple Translate and Google Translate offer camera-based alternatives; Spek offers a photo-and-result workflow.

- **Menu in front of you:** try [Camera’s Live Text](#apple-camera).
- **Photo or screenshot already saved:** use [Photos](#apple-photos).
- **Want translations over the camera view:** try [Apple Translate](#apple-translate) or [Google Translate](#google-translate).
- **Want to read a translation alongside your chosen image in Spek:** use [Spek’s Image tab](#translate-a-menu).

Check that your menu’s language and the language you want to read are supported by the method you choose. Reading a few dish names does not require the same workflow as translating a whole page.

<h2 id="apple-tools">Use Apple’s built-in tools</h2>

<h3 id="apple-camera">1. Translate the menu in Camera</h3>

Use this when you want to understand a word or a short section without taking a photo.

1. Open **Camera** and point it at the menu.
2. Wait for the text-detection frame, then tap the **Detect Text / Live Text** button.
3. Select the words you want to understand and choose **Translate**.

For the current controls, see [Apple’s Camera instructions](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/use-live-text-iphcf0b71b0e/ios). Availability varies by language and region.

<h3 id="apple-photos">2. Translate a saved menu in Photos</h3>

Use this for a menu photo you took earlier or a screenshot someone sent you.

1. Open the image in **Photos**.
2. Tap **Live Text** to detect its text.
3. Tap **Translate**. Follow any prompt to continue or change the translation language.

You can also select specific words when you only need one dish explained. Apple lists an **iPhone XS, iPhone XR, or later with iOS 15 or later** for Live Text. Its [Photos guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120004) includes the requirements and steps.

<h3 id="apple-translate">3. Use the camera in Apple Translate</h3>

If you prefer translations displayed over the menu image, try Apple’s **Translate** app.

1. Open **Translate**, then choose **Camera**.
2. Point it at the menu and check the language choices.
3. Freeze the view with the shutter button, then zoom in to inspect the translated text.

You can also select a saved photo from the camera view. This works for supported languages; [Apple’s Translate guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/translate-with-the-camera-iphea8b95631/ios) explains the available controls. If a built-in method meets your needs, you can stop here.

<h2 id="google-translate">Try Google Translate</h2>

Google Translate is another option if you already use it or want to check a different app’s language support.

1. Open **Google Translate** on your iPhone.
2. Choose the menu’s language, or **Detect language**, and your target language.
3. Tap **Camera** and point it at the menu. Tap the shutter to pause, or use **Gallery** for a saved image.

Google also documents **offline camera translation**: download both languages through the camera’s language picker before you need them. Check that downloads are available for your language pair and try the workflow before leaving Wi-Fi. See [Google’s iPhone instructions](https://support.google.com/translate/answer/6142483?co=GENIE.Platform%3DiOS&hl=en).

<h2 id="translate-a-menu">Translate a menu photo in Spek</h2>

Spek is our app. If you prefer to choose a photo and read the translated text alongside the original, here is its [photo translation workflow]({{ '/#photo-translation' | relative_url }}):

1. Open **Image** in Spek. Choose **Camera** or **Photo Library** and select a clear menu image.
2. Set **From** to the menu’s language and **To** to your reading language. Choose different languages.
3. Tap **Translate Image**, then review the result with the original. Use **Change Image** to translate another section.

Spek requires an internet connection, and some features require an in-app purchase. Check its [language and dialect choices]({{ '/#dialect-choices' | relative_url }}) for your needs. You can [find Spek on the App Store]({{ site.appstore_link }}) if this workflow suits you.

<h2 id="clearer-results">When the result isn’t useful</h2>

<h3>Live Text or Translate is missing</h3>

Check **Settings → General → Language & Region → Live Text**. Confirm that your device and language are supported. If text selection does not show **Translate**, look in the selection menu’s additional options. Apple describes those options in its [text translation guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/translate-text-in-apps-iphab4dcff1d/ios).

<details class="guide-tips">
  <summary>If the text is hard to read</summary>
  <ul><li>Keep the menu flat and avoid reflections, glare, or deep shadows.</li><li>Photograph one section at a time so small text stays legible.</li><li>Retake a blurry image and check the source and target languages.</li><li>If a dish name still makes little sense, ask the restaurant what it contains.</li></ul>
</details>

<h3>A QR code opens a digital menu</h3>

If it opens a webpage in **Safari**, look in the **Page Menu** for **Translate to [language]** when available. For an image or screenshot, try the Photos method instead. [Apple’s Safari guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/browse-the-web-iph1fbef4daa/ios) covers webpage translation; it is not available for every language or region.

<h3>You won’t have mobile data</h3>

Prepare and test an offline option before your trip. Google documents downloadable languages for camera translation, as described above. Spek needs an internet connection, so it is not an offline option for this task.

Use translated menus to help you choose, but confirm ingredients and preparation with restaurant staff if you have an allergy or another dietary requirement. A translated dish name alone cannot establish what is in the meal.
