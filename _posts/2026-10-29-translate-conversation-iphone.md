<h2 id="quick-setup">How do you translate a conversation with one iPhone?</h2>

**Choose both languages, open a conversation or voice screen, and speak one short turn at a time. Let the translation finish before the other person replies.** Apple Translate, Google Translate, and Spek offer different ways to manage those turns.

- **Start with Apple Translate** if its supported languages meet your needs.
- **Try Google Translate** if you want its current Live translate modes, including spoken Conversation and Text only.
- **Consider Spek** if you want an explicit country choice and a hold-to-speak, release-to-translate workflow.

Put the phone where both people can read it and the microphone can pick up the current speaker. Confirm who speaks which language, set a comfortable volume, and ask the other person to pause while the translated result plays.

This guide covers people talking together in the same place. For a phone call, FaceTime call, or AirPods listening session, [check the separate requirements below](#calls-and-airpods).

<h2 id="apple-conversation">Apple Translate: use Conversation and Face to Face</h2>

Apple’s [conversation guide](https://support.apple.com/en-euro/guide/iphone/n4pjjxr31uel/ios) documents a shared-phone workflow:

1. Open **Translate** and tap **Conversation**. Check the two languages you want to use.
2. Tap **Listen**, then say a short phrase. You can also use **Enter text** to type or paste a message.
3. Read the result and tap **Play** to hear it.
4. Give the other person time to answer, then review their translated reply.

For easier reading across a table, choose **View → Face to Face**. Under **More Options**, Apple also documents **Auto Translate** for detecting spoken turns without repeatedly tapping the microphone, and **Play Translations** for automatic audio playback.

Check Apple’s [language availability](https://www.apple.com/ios/feature-availability/) before relying on a particular pair. If the available languages and controls fit your task, you may not need another app.

<h2 id="google-conversation">Google Translate: choose the right Live translate mode</h2>

Google’s current [iPhone instructions](https://support.google.com/translate/answer/6142474?co=GENIE.Platform%3DiOS&hl=en) cover Live translate with or without headphones. Update the app before looking for these controls.

1. Open **Google Translate** and tap **Live translate** at the bottom.
2. Select the languages and choose **Conversation** for translations played through the phone’s speaker or connected headphones.
3. Take turns speaking. Wait for each result before continuing.
4. Tap **Speak** to pause or resume, and **Back** to leave the session.

Choose **Text only** when you want to read without audio. **Face to face** gives each speaker their own half of the screen. Google also lists a Listening mode, but some options depend on connected headphones; choose the mode that matches sharing one phone.

If you only need a single phrase, Google separately documents [Dictation](https://support.google.com/translate/answer/6142468?co=GENIE.Platform%3DiOS&hl=en): select the languages, tap Dictation, and speak after the tone. A disabled control can mean the selected spoken language is unavailable.

<h2 id="spek-conversation">Spek: hold, release, review, and swap</h2>

Spek is our app. Its [voice workflow]({{ '/#features' | relative_url }}) gives you direct control over when a spoken turn ends. It requires an internet connection and offers in-app purchases.

1. Open the voice screen and tap the language header.
2. Set your spoken language as the source and the other person’s language as the target. Select the relevant country where offered.
3. Wait for the connection to be ready. **Hold the microphone while speaking**, then release it to request translation.
4. Review the recognized words and the translated result. Where the speaker control is available, use it to replay audio.
5. **Swap the languages** before the other person’s turn. They then hold the microphone, speak, and release it.

If you press while the connection is still being established, wait until it is ready and try again. Holding the microphone for a clear spoken phrase matters; a silent or very short capture may not request a result.

For regional language choices, see our guides to [Egyptian Arabic]({{ '/blog/egyptian-arabic-translator-iphone/' | relative_url }}), [Mexican Spanish]({{ '/blog/mexican-spanish-translator-iphone/' | relative_url }}), and [Portuguese for Portugal]({{ '/blog/european-portuguese-translator-iphone/' | relative_url }}). A country choice requests that variety; it does not establish that every output is accurate or appropriate for the situation.

Spek’s controls described here manage spoken turns on the voice screen. They do not provide phone-call translation or unattended background interpretation. Check the [current App Store listing]({{ site.appstore_link }}) when deciding whether its features fit your needs.

<h2 id="better-turns">Get a clearer reply before changing apps</h2>

Many confusing results begin before translation: the app heard the wrong words, picked up a nearby conversation, or missed which person was speaking.

**Keep one idea in each turn.** Ask where the entrance is, wait for the answer, then ask about opening time. Several questions spoken together make it harder to tell which answer belongs to which request.

**Inspect the recognized sentence.** If you said a hotel name or street number, check it on the screen. Repeat an unclear name or type it rather than asking the app to translate a mistaken transcription again.

**Let playback finish.** Wait before recording the reply so the phone’s speaker does not become part of the next spoken turn. If the space is noisy, move closer together or show the translated text.

**Rephrase an unclear result.** Use a complete, straightforward sentence with the relevant context. Keep times, amounts, and destinations explicit. Translating the same ambiguous phrase repeatedly may preserve the ambiguity.

**Check the language direction.** After a reply, confirm which language the next speaker will use. This is particularly useful in workflows where you swap the source and target manually.

<h3>A short practice task before your trip</h3>

With a willing partner, ask which entrance to use at a fictional hotel. Have them give one direction, then ask a follow-up about the time it opens. Check that you can both read the results, hear playback, and take turns without talking over the phone.

Use this as a setup check. It does not prove translation accuracy, and we have not presented it as a recorded app test. When the wording matters, ask a fluent speaker whether the translated message conveys your intended meaning.

<h2 id="offline-preparation">Prepare before leaving Wi-Fi</h2>

Apple documents [downloaded languages](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/translate-words-and-phrases-iphd74cb450f/ios) and [on-device translation settings](https://support.apple.com/en-euro/guide/iphone/iphddb6e7264/ios). Download both required languages, then test Conversation with an actual spoken turn and playback in airplane mode before relying on it.

Do the same practical check for whichever app, language pair, and input method you plan to use. Do not infer that a downloaded text language also makes every live voice mode available offline. Spek needs internet, so arrange a working connection if you choose it.

Keep essential addresses and booking details saved as text, too. A written fallback can help when the microphone, connection, or noisy surroundings interrupt the conversation.

<h2 id="common-questions">Common questions</h2>

<h3>Can two people use the same iPhone?</h3>

Yes. Each workflow above can support a shared-phone exchange. Keep the current speaker clear and allow time to read or hear the result.

<h3 id="calls-and-airpods">Is this the same as translating a call or using AirPods?</h3>

Those are separate workflows. Apple documents [Live Translation in Phone and FaceTime](https://support.apple.com/en-us/123720) with their own controls and language availability. Its [AirPods translation guide](https://support.apple.com/en-euro/guide/airpods/dev9c215ca94/web) requires compatible AirPods and an Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone, with software, language, and region requirements. Check those requirements specifically if you want in-ear listening. You do not need to buy AirPods just to try the shared-phone methods above.

<h3>Which conversation translator is most accurate?</h3>

This guide does not rank accuracy. Start with a tool that supports the languages and controls you need, check the recognized words, and review the result in context. Automatic turn detection and a natural-sounding voice cannot guarantee that a message is correct.
